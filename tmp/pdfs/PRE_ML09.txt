Embeddings
• Embeddings are vector representations of a 
data object. The data object can be words 
tokens, patches of images, or chucks of 
audio files. 
• Items are represented as vectors, which are 
the “coordinates” in the embedding space. 
For example,  vector [1, -3]  are the [x, y] 
coordinates on a two-dimensional space.
• An embedding space is a high-dimensional 
space used to represent text, words, or 
other types of data in a way that captures 
the semantic relationships between them.

The Power of Embeddings + Transformers
6
Previously, generation of embedding spaces was limited to sequential, one-way processing 
capturing linear textual relationships
Transformers utilize the context of words in a sentence to generate dynamic embeddings 
that represent nuanced meanings, differentiating between multiple uses of the same word. 
1.  The crisp apple is my favorite fruit to enjoy as a healthy snack.
2.  Apple has revolutionized the tech industry with its innovative products like the iPhone and MacBook.
3.  I love munching on potato chips while watching my favorite movie.
4. The new computer is equipped with high-performance chips that enhance its processing speed and efficiency.

Sentence Transformers ≠  GPTs
8
Sentence Transformers
GPTs (Generative Pre-trained 
Transformer)
• Primarily designed to produce fixed-
size sentence embeddings, which can 
be utilized for tasks like semantic 
textual similarity, information retrieval, 
and clustering. 
• Sentence Transformers excels at 
understanding relationships between 
sentences and measuring their 
similarity.
• Designed for generative tasks, such as 
text generation, conversation, and 
creative writing. GPT models can 
produce coherent and contextually 
relevant text outputs based on 
prompts, making them suitable for 
applications like chatbots, 
summarization, and content creation.

• Vector DB: 
    Efficient data processing is crucial for applications involving large language models, generative AI, and semantic search.

not limited to : Similarity Comparisons, Fast Approximate K-Nearest Neighbor, Metadata Storage & Retrieval.

Challenges
• More studies are involved , more time-consuming it becomes.  [44 studies/ protocols]
• Large number of ADSLs variables, and even larger number of SDTM datasets/variables to search through.

• Variations in the organization of information in SDTM domains across different studies/protocols, causing challenges to 
determine which SDTM domain dataset contains the relevant information.
• Variations in codelist/ variable values.

PAGE 31
MRR score for ADSL mapping 
31
• The Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) for all queries is 0.97. This indicates that in nearly every instance, the correct matching 
variable is consistently placed at the highest rank. Or, when there is no correct variable within the top 3 list, it is true 
that the information is absent from the ADSL dataset.
• For queries where variable name/label not the exact same, the MRR is impressively high at 0.82. This indicates that, in a 
significant majority of these cases, the appropriate matching variable is ranked either first or second. 
• Overall, these metrics demonstrate the robustness and efficiency of the semantic search algorithm in retrieving 
pertinent information, even in circumstances where exact matching is lacking.

Discussion
32
• Enhanced Efficiency and Accuracy
Semantic search offers statistical programmers a systematic and reliable approach to accurately locate source variables 
and datasets. 
This is particularly beneficial when dealing with data integration requests that involve 1) large number of studies, or 2) 
partners’ clinical data, where the complexity increases due to varying terminologies and data structures.
By employing semantic search, the time required to identify the correct variables and datasets is significantly reduced. 
As a result, data integration projects can achieve a “jump start”,  allowing project team to identify potential issues 
sooner. This early identification encourages discussion and the development of solutions to challenges, ultimately 
improving overall project efficiency.

Discussion
33
• Empowering Statistical Programmers
The semantic search only provides recommendations for variable and dataset selection. 
It is statistical programmers’ responsibility to review the recommendations and make the final judgements based on 
their domain expertise and adhere to their company’s SOPs. 
This human-in-the-loop approach ensures that decisions are grounded in practical experience, thereby enhancing the 
quality of data integration.
• Leveraging Advanced Technologies
The advancements in semantic search—from embedding models to vector databases and search algorithms—represent 
a well-explored area with many available ready-to-use products. 
However, the successful application of these technologies in variable mapping hinges on the statistical programmers’ 
domain knowledge and experience in key areas such as SDTM, ADaM , and the overall data integration process.
