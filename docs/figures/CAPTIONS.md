# Publication figure captions

**Figure 1. From clinical evidence to synthetic trial data.** (A) Data-quality controls preserve source provenance, denominators and clinical context as historical clinical sources are converted into statistical observations and fitted probability tables. The evidence foundation contains 1,000 trials, 2,771 clinical profiles, 106,185 observations and 4,693 fitted parameters. (B) Protocol specifications provide eligibility, visit, treatment and endpoint rules; probability tables supply distributions for population generation and trial simulation. Missing eligibility information remains explicit. (C) Simulation produces synthetic records and feasibility estimates, followed by comparison of frozen predictions with retrieved registry outcomes. Every row reads from left to right; the return connector links trial simulation to its outputs. Recruitment and event-specific safety models use additional registry evidence. Source links, assumptions and unresolved quantities accompany the outputs.

**Figure 2. From an observed count to a probability model.** The running example is any serious adverse event among participants receiving temozolomide for melanoma in NCT00091572. (A) The reported count of 124 affected participants among 419 at risk becomes a statistical observation that preserves seriousness, attribution and the safety denominator. One study directly supports the selected parameter, with six additional studies supporting borrowing. (B) The within-study posterior, hierarchical population posterior and future-study probability distribution answer different questions. Points and horizontal intervals show medians and 95% intervals; the dashed line marks the observed proportion. The within-study curve is a Beta posterior; the population and future-study curves are smoothed from 400 stored draws each. The latter describes uncertainty in a new study's event probability, before sampling a finite participant count. (C) The share of posterior precision contributed by broader evidence is shown at each hierarchical level. These percentages are model diagnostics, not proportions of borrowed patients.

**Figure 3. Applying a protocol to a synthetic cohort.** ACNS0332 is a development example whose outcomes were known during model development. (A) Forty-one extracted screening criteria are classified by evaluability and purpose. Missing patient variables and unresolved rules leave all 10,000 screened patients undetermined; none is confirmed eligible or ineligible. (B) Simulated enrolment under protocol scenarios of 35 and 60 participants per year reaches the 400-participant target in approximately 10.6 and 6.2 years, respectively. (C) The control-arm event-free survival curve is shown with the predicted five-year value and 90% interval, alongside the registry estimate and its 95% confidence interval. The different interval levels are retained explicitly.

**Figure 4. Prediction freeze and blinded evaluation.** The sequence shows prediction freeze, registry-result retrieval and subsequent evaluation. The chronology table reports the final prediction-stage freeze, registry retrieval and first comparison-record timestamps for NCT04003610 (Study 1), NCT04205799 (Study 2) and NCT04083170 (Study 3). All times are UTC on 28 September 2026. All ten prediction stages preceded retrieval in each study. The final prediction was frozen 35 seconds, 117 minutes 17 seconds and 84 minutes 56 seconds before retrieval, respectively. The table establishes the recorded order of these operations; timestamps alone do not demonstrate absence of all possible information leakage.

**Figure 5. Recruitment forecasts and observed trial outcomes.** (A) Frozen recruitment-rate forecasts are shown as medians with 50% and 80% predictive intervals on a logarithmic scale. Diamonds mark protocol assumptions where available. Right-pointing markers show observed lower bounds, calculated from enrolment divided by the registry start-to-primary-completion window. Study 3 enrolled one participant and is not evaluated for recruitment rate. (B) For each study, the model probability of the outcome that occurred is compared with its historical base rate; adjacent values are model probability / base rate. The observed outcomes were termination for another reason (Study 1), completion (Study 2) and termination for poor accrual (Study 3). Study identifiers are defined in Figure 4.

**Figure 6. Component calibration and predictive performance.** (A) Empirical interval coverage is reported at each available nominal level, with the evaluation-unit count for each component. Dashes indicate an unavailable interval level. Evaluation units and validation designs differ across components; adverse-event observations are not independent trials. Survival summaries constitute an internal consistency check because the evaluated trial contributes to the hazard-ratio posterior. Coverage should be interpreted together with interval width and predictive score, not as a stand-alone measure of usefulness. (B) Mean log predictive probability for listed adverse-event counts is shown for an in-sample reference model and for the subsequent model before and after calibration. The latter uses separate fitting, calibration and reporting partitions. Higher scores are better; the in-sample reference is not a held-out benchmark.

**Figure 7. Event-level safety predictions in the blinded studies.** All matched reported event comparisons are shown for Study 1 (NCT04003610; seven participants at risk) and Study 2 (NCT04205799; 54 at risk). Predicted count medians, 90% predictive intervals and registry counts are divided by the corresponding registry population at risk to place them on a proportion scale. Ten of ten and 31 of 33 comparisons, respectively, lie inside their intervals. Orange diamonds identify observations outside the interval. Seriousness and grade qualifiers distinguish repeated event terms; ALT and AST denote alanine and aspartate aminotransferase. Unlisted events are excluded from this figure because their absence from a registry table need not imply zero affected participants. Broad intervals and correlated events limit interpretation of the high coverage.

**Figure 8. Aggregate safety and control-survival estimates.** (A, B) Estimates of the proportion experiencing any serious adverse event are shown for the selected subgroup and the remaining age, phase and drug-class subgroups. Points are subgroup estimates; thick intervals are 95% confidence intervals for subgroup means; thin intervals are 80% ranges for a new trial. Study counts indicate the supporting evidence. Different drug classes means no class overlap with the protocol regimen. Vertical lines and shaded bands show observed proportions and their 95% Wilson confidence intervals. Selected-subgroup estimates were 58.8% versus 2/7 (28.6%) observed in Study 1, and 14.5% versus 26/54 (48.1%) in Study 2. (C) For Study 1, the historical control-arm median progression-free survival estimate was 4.5 months, supported by three studies, compared with 2.1 months among six registry control-arm participants. The registry interval has no reached upper limit, represented by an arrow. These comparisons expose disagreement in point estimates; they do not establish synthetic control-arm equivalence.

## Production and traceability

Each figure is supplied as a vector PDF, an editable-text SVG and a 600-dpi PNG at 7.2 inches wide. Publication labels omit software versions, internal row and parameter identifiers, source-code names and checksums. Study registry identifiers, sample sizes and scientific qualifiers are retained. The companion `figure_provenance.json` records source files, checksums and the running example's internal identifiers.

Regenerate from the project root with `.venv-figures/Scripts/python.exe scripts/paper_figures.py`. No source observations or predictions are modified by figure generation.
